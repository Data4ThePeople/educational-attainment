```
On October 29, after a delay of about six weeks, the Census Bureau finally released the results of its 1-year American Community Survey for 2025. Over the weekend, we rolled up our sleeves and dug in, starting with a topic we haven't yet explored: educational attainment.

On its own, the data is quite encouraging. The share of adults 25 and over with a bachelor's degree or more rose from 30.6% to 37.4% between 2015 and 2025.

So that's fantastic. Story over?

Not so fast. If educational attainment is changing this much, can we compare pay from one decade to the next without adjusting for it? More adults are now in the groups with higher pay. In finance terms, that is a more favorable mix.

Nationally, about half of the growth in our inflation-adjusted pay index lines up with Americans having more education. But that split varies widely from one metro area to the next. In the New York City metro area, pay within education groups fell 0.20% a year, and all of the growth came from the change in education mix.

So the next time you see a headline that pay is up, ask the coffee shop question: was it price, or was it mix?

The map and the full report are linked in the first comment.
```
